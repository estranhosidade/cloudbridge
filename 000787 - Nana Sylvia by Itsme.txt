Nana Sylvia

"Do I have to do it?" I asked my wife Holly.

"Yes, Nana Sylvia has been very good to us. If it wasn't for her financial support we might be out on the street. She even throws in some extra so we can have the niceties of life."

"I know, but can't you do it? She's your grandmother. I understand why she wanted to be young again, but I'm a man."

"She doesn't want to be alone. It's not just she wants to be young, but spend some time with me too."

I complained, "This is all very weird."

"I know. But, all you have to do is swap places with her for two days. That doesn't sound like a lot for all she has given us."

"OK, I just. I wonder what other husbands who would spend two days in their body of their wife's grandmother."

"Stop complaining. This isn't the first time. It'll be the same as before. It'll be over before you know it."

I get into my side of the bed and make myself comfortable. Tomorrow when I wake up, I won't be the 25-year-old man I am. I'll be in the body of Silvia, my grandmother-in-law. I'll be a woman who is 88-years-old. I'll have all her ailments and live in a nursing home. If I hadn't lost my job, I wouldn't be in this problem. But that is a different story. One that wasn't entirely my fault. I finally fall asleep. Only two days and I'll be back to being myself.

The soft chime of my alarm clock gently wakes me. There is a half-remembered dream in my head, I'm in the arms of someone. I can't remember it all, I blink a few times, trying to clear the fog in my mind. When I'm Sylvia, I don't think as fast as I used to. Holly told me there wasn't anything wrong with Sylvia's mind. That it's just the disorientation of being in her body that is confusing me. But, I'm not so sure.

The room is bathed in soft morning light, and I feel the familiar ache in my joints. A moment later, the door opens, and Maria, my caregiver, walks in with her bright smile. Every time, I'm Sylvia, Maria enters my room with that same bright smile.

"Good morning, Sylvia! How are you feeling today?" Maria asks me.

I smile back, though it feels a bit stiff. "Morning, Maria. I think I slept well."

I dare not tell Maria that I'm not really Sylvia. That I'm her grandson-in-law spending two days in her body. If I told her, would she think me a hero (for helping her out) or pathetic (for allowing myself to be this way)?

Maria helps me sit up and puts my thick glasses on my nose. The world comes into clearer focus, though the memories sometimes feel just out of reach. Sometimes it feels as though I can remember parts of Sylvia's life. I don't know if they are true memories or what I would imagine her life would be. I've seen photographs of Sylvia when she was young...well younger. Maybe that is what I am recalling. The memory of me holding in my arms my future wife when she was a baby, seems so real and faded at the same time.

Maria adjusts my hearing aids, and the sounds of the world become more distinct. After helping me freshen up, she guides me to the bathroom. I stand holding the railing, while she bends down, reaches under my nightgown, and pulls down my pull-up. She holds up the hem of my gown and helps me to sit on the toilet.

I know I have to go, but it takes quite a while before I can do anything. Maria waits patiently as do I. There isn't anything either of us can do to hurry this along. We just have to wait. My mind wanders. I smile when I muse about what Maria would do if I started to touch myself. I might try it, if my fingers didn't already ache. To pass the time, Maria tells me of what she has been up to when she isn't at work.

Later, Maria helps me to brush my teeth and fit my dentures into place. Maria's gentle hands make everything easier.

Once I'm settled back in my chair, Maria moves to my wardrobe. "What would you like to wear today, Sylvia? It's a bit sunny outside, so maybe something light and comfortable."

I appreciate Maria giving me the choice. Choosing the feminine apparel I'm about to wear still feels a bit odd to me. Her suggestions always help. "That sounds good. Maybe the blue dress with the flowers?"

Maria nods approvingly. "Great choice! It's one of my favorites on you."

I smile. Somehow reaffirming my choice makes me feel good. She doesn't suspect that I'm not really Sylvia. But, I wonder if she says that to the real Sylvia.

She takes out the dress and lays it gently on the bed. Then she fetches a pair of soft, stretchy leggings to go underneath, knowing my arthritis makes me feel chilly sometimes. She also picks out a light cardigan in case I need an extra layer.

It's almost summer outside. My real body wouldn't wear this many layers of clothing. But in this body, any little breeze gives me a chill.

Maria helps me stand, steadying me with her arm. My joints protest, but her support gives me confidence. Slowly, we make our way to the bed, where I sit down. Maria helps me take off my nightgown, her movements careful and respectful.

"Let's start with the leggings," she says, guiding each of my legs through the fabric. She pulls them up gently, making sure they fit comfortably around my waist. I look down. My tummy is round and flat. There isn't a hint of manhood there. Or any other place on my body.

Next, she helps me into the dress. I lift my arms as much as I can, and Maria carefully slips the dress over my head, guiding my arms through the sleeves. The fabric feels soft against my skin. She smooths out the wrinkles and makes sure it sits right.

"How about these shoes?" Maria holds up a pair of comfortable, slip-on shoes. "They're easy to put on and will be good for walking."

"If the ambling I do could really be called walking." I think but don't say.

I nod in agreement. She helps me into the shoes, making sure they're secure with the Velcro straps but not too tight.

Maria then brings the light cardigan, helping me slip it on. "There, all set! You look lovely, Sylvia."

"This is the most lovely I have felt in weeks," I quip. Maria doesn't know that the last time I was in Sylvia's body was weeks ago.

I look at myself in the mirror, and despite the aches and the foggy memories, I feel a sense of satisfaction. "Thank you, Maria. You always know just what to do and say."

Maria smiles warmly. "It's my pleasure, Sylvia. Ready for breakfast?"

With Maria's help, I stand and she assists me into my wheelchair. As she wheels me towards the dining room, I feel grateful for her care and the comfort of a familiar routine. The day is off to a good start, thanks to Maria's kindness and patience.

The aroma of oatmeal and honey makes my mouth water. I enjoy the warmth of the tea in my hands, feeling the steam rise up and warm my face. I take my first bite. Soft foods are all my dentures can handle. As far as the taste goes, I don't know if the food is really too bland or if my taste buds like the rest of my body has softened.

During breakfast, Maria gave me a cup with several pills in it. "Time for your medication," she tells me.

I didn't know what any of the pills did, but I dutifully took them one at a time.

Maria wheels me into the day room. There are many people there. Most of the residents are women, but there are also a few men. I can't remember their names, but it doesn't matter.

"Oh, there's that handsome gentleman again, sitting with his friends. They're always so engrossed in their shows. He has such a dignified presence, doesn't he? I wonder what they're watching today," I whisper to Maria.

Maria smiles at me. She knew which of the gentlemen I was referring to. I thought they were all handsome. "Yes, Samuel is very handsome," she agrees.

Did I say handsome? I don't know what I was thinking. Maybe this body has affected me more than I thought it would. I'll try and stay with the women.

After breakfast, Mark the physical therapist, arrives. Mark hopefully is the only man I will see while I'm here. Most of the residents and virtually all the staff are women. 

Mark always has a kind word and a gentle touch. We work through some stretches and exercises, which help ease the stiffness in my joints. It's hard work, but I know it helps.

Next, I join a few other residents in the common area for a crafting session. I can either craft with the others or sit and watch soap opera reruns from the 1970s. The choices are limited and all femininely directed.

Today, we're working with soft fabrics. The textures are soothing, and I enjoy the creative process, even if my hands don't always do what I want them to.

Lunch is a social time. I have mashed potatoes, steamed vegetables, and some soft fish. The fish has been deboned of course. The conversation at the table is lively (as lively as it gets with women in a nursing home), though sometimes I have to ask people to repeat themselves because of my hearing. The food is easy to eat and tastes good. It is tastier than the oatmeal of this morning. I should have asked Maria to add more sugar, though.

During lunch, Maria gave me a cup with several pills in it. "Time for your medication," she told me.

"I already had my medication at breakfast," I reminded her.

"These are your afternoon pills."

"Oh."

I took these pills too.

After lunch, I feel tired and return to my room for a nap. My god, I've only been up five or six hours, and I'm already exhausted. Maria helps me settle into a comfortable chair by the window. I pick up a large print book, though I find myself reading the same page several times. I dozed off during my reading session.

I was startled awake by a woman talking to me. Maria was gone and my book was in my lap. I didn't know who this person was, but I knew from her attire that the young well-endowed woman worked for the home. "Good afternoon, Sylvia! How are you feeling today?"

"Hello," I said, but I was annoyed that she woke me.

"How are you feeling?"

"Umm, I think OK," I told her.

"You don't know who I am?"

I didn't know whether to admit that I didn't or not. I decided to hedge. "You work for the home."

"I'm Jane. I'm an occupational therapist. I'm here to help you."

"Oh, I'm sorry. I didn't recognize you. My hands are really stiff today." My hands were stiff, but I didn't know if this was worse or better than it had been. I had only been Sylvia for a few hours.

Jane nodded understandingly. "I'm sorry to hear that. Let's see what we can do to help. We'll start with some gentle exercises to warm up your hands and improve your mobility."

Jane moved a small table closer to my chair and placed a variety of tools on it: soft therapy putty, small sponges, and a bowl of warm water. She began by guiding me through some simple hand exercises.

"First, let's dip your hands in this warm water. It will help relax the muscles and ease the stiffness," she explained.

I placed my hands in the water, feeling the soothing heat penetrate my aching joints. After a few minutes, Jane helped me dry my hands and then introduced the therapy putty.

"Now, I want you to squeeze this putty, just a little at a time. It's great for strengthening your hand muscles," Jane told me.

The therapy lasted for over a half hour. I don't know if it improved the pain in my hands. I'll be happy to get out of here and into my own body. Next time, when I'm " me again." I couldn't remember my own name. "I'll put my foot down, I'm not going to swap lives with Sylvia again."

The therapy was seemingly cut short. Maria entered the room while I was doing one exercise or the other. "Dr. Morris wants to see you."

I knew that Dr. Alisa Morris was one of the psychiatrists that worked at the home. I didn't mind seeing her. Especially if it would end the torture that was call occupational therapy.

"Good afternoon, Sylvia. I wanted to check in on you to see if the new medication was working." Dr. Morris asked.

"New medication? I take so many."

"I know. But, you remember we started you on hormone therapy. As I explained, research has been done that indicates that certain sex hormones help diminish memory loss."

"I remember," I lied.

"But, although they help with that, they have a tendency of increasing a person's libido."

I nodded.

"So how is it going? Do you feel more lucid?"

"I'm not sure," I told her truthfully. I had only been in Sylvia's body for a few hours. I didn't know if my thinking was clearer or not. But, I knew that I wasn't as quick as I was when I was in my own body. That's for sure.

"I see." Dr. Morris then smiled and asked, "You aren't attacking any men are you?"

"No, I think I can keep my hands off of them." I realized that it must be the medication that was affecting me. I didn't worry about my thoughts of the men anymore. I would be back to normal once I returned to my own body.

"Good. But I'll tell you what we are going to do. Let's increase the dose a little and see what happens."

"I'm not sure."

"Don't worry about it, we can decrease it later if we need to."

I didn't think I could object. I would be gone from this body soon anyway.

"If you think that is best," I told the doctor.

She produced a small paper cup the size of a shot glass which contained a red fluid. "Take this," Dr. Morris instructed.

I drank it down. It tasted like cherry syrup. Dr. Morris continued her interview. I answered the best I could to all her questions. Maria was called and I was returned to the day room. I was helped onto the corner of the couch in front of the television. I didn't want to sit with the ladies anymore. Television seemed more enjoyable.

It wasn't long before I spotted Samuel enter the room. He wasn't with his friends. He needed a cane, but other than that, he could walk unassisted by the staff. He sat next to me.

"Hey, beautiful."

I knew what was happening. I knew I was under the effect of the drug. I didn't care. I liked being called beautiful, as long as he was the one saying it. Besides without help, I couldn't get up. I also discovered than I didn't want to. I liked being close to him.

"Samuel, hello handsome," I felt it was only appropriate to respond in kind.

"What are you watching?"

"An old movie. I forgot it's name."

"Mind if I watch it with you?"

"Of course not. I would prefer company."

"Ow," Samuel exclaimed, before extending his arms. "These stiff joined of mine," he continued. Then he wrapped his arm around my shoulders and drew me in close. I allowed myself to be manipulated. It felt good. I knew I was under the influence of the drug, but I didn't care. I even snuggled closer as we watched the show. We sat close together, until Samuel had to leave to engage in one of his scheduled therapies. I missed him when he left me.

Emma, my daughter (actually my mother-in-law), visited in the late afternoon. She brought flowers and homemade cookies that are soft enough for me to eat. We look through photo albums and talk about the past. I tell Emma that I forgot some names and places, but Emma is patient and helps me 'remember'. I was surprised when she showed me a recent picture of Silvia, Holly and myself.

"Who is that with Holly?" I asked Emma.

"That's her husband Trevor," she told me. My own face didn't seem familiar.

"Yes, I remember now." I lied. That did it. I'm never swapping with Sylvia again.

Dinner is light- - - vegetable soup and bread rolls. The warmth of the soup is soothing. After dinner, there's a gentle exercise class in the common area. The movements are simple, and I can do them while seated. It feels good to move, even if just a little. Despite wearing the cardigan, I didn't make it outside. I was in the home all day.

As the day ends, Maria comes to help me with my nighttime routine. She helps me change into my nightgown, takes me to the bathroom, and puts me into another diaper. Technically they are pull-ups. Although the nighttime pull-ups are thicker than the ones I wear during the day. She carefully removes my hearing aids, glasses, and dentures, cleaning and storing them properly. She helps me into bed, arranging my pillows and blankets just right.

I lie in bed, feeling a sense of gratitude for the care I receive. The day has been filled with small moments of joy and kindness. They do take care of their charges very well here. If I was in Sylvia's condition for real, this is the place I would want to live.

As I close my eyes, the gentle hum of the night lulls me into a peaceful sleep. Only one more day of this and I'll be back to my own body. I'll never be Sylvia again after that. 

As I sleep, I have dreams of Samuel and me. We are still elderly people, but the pains and the infirmities of the present have gone. We can do much more than snuggle. A smile appears on my face.

Holly and Trevor (her husband) enters my room as I'm sleeping. They watch me sleeping peacefully. They can see my look of contentment and a broad toothless grin.

Trevor says to Holly, "You think he knows he has been here for over a month?"

Holly shakes her head.

"It's a nice place. They do take care of you. Look at that smile. I wonder what he is dreaming about?"

"Probably that therapy nurse. Have you seen the jugs on that woman?"

End.
