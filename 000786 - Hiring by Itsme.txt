Hiring

< Hiring.jpg >

Mrs. Abreu used the Atazacron to transform her husband's mind. She didn't remove his entire mind, just his personality and identity. He didn't think of himself as a dominant man or a captain of industry. He saw himself as an older woman named Lydia who was on 'her' own for the first time. He didn't remember that he had been married to the woman who sat before him, Mrs. Abreu. He believed he had been a wife and a mother of three children. Mrs. Abreu would hire him eventually, but she would make him suffer. She wanted to see her husband beg to work as a maid in a house that he had once owned.

Mrs. Abreu met Lydia at the door. He was right on time. "I'm Mrs. Abreu." She extended her arm with her hand loosely hanging from her wrist. Lydia shook it.

"I'm Lydia."

"Lydia, I hope you don't mind, but this is the uniform you will be expected to wear. Would you mind putting it on for the interview?"

Lydia took the uniform and changed in the bathroom. The uniform consisted of a yellow and white dress with teal accents, white tights, and white rubber sole shoes. He put on the costume, and then Mrs. Abreu purposefully made him sit in a seat too short for him. She wanted to grind all resistance out of him.

Lydia, with a worn expression, sat across from Mrs. Abreu, his hands wrapped around his knees. The small office feels stifling as he adjusts his posture, determined to appear calm and collected despite the anxiety gnawing at his insides.

"So," Mrs. Abreu begins, her eyes scanning Lydia's skimpy resume, (a resume that Mrs. Abreu wrote herself) "I see you don't have any formal experience working as a maid. Why should I consider you for this position?"

Lydia swallowed hard but forced a smile. "That's true, ma'am. I haven't worked as a maid before, but I've spent my entire life running my household." Lydia hadn't any memory of doing anything other than being a homemaker. "I raised three children and took care of all the cleaning, cooking, and organizing. I'm familiar with every aspect of keeping a home in order---vacuuming, scrubbing floors, washing windows, doing laundry, ironing, even managing the grocery shopping."

Mrs. Abreu narrowed her eyes. She was enjoying this. "But you've never worked for someone else. This is a different environment, Lydia. You'll be handling other people's possessions, following their specific instructions. How can I be sure you'll meet those expectations?" Mrs. Abreu stressed the words, "Other People's Possessions".

Lydia's heart pounded, but he tried to keep his voice steady. "I understand that it's different. But I'm a fast learner, and I'm careful. I know how important it is to respect someone's home and their belongings. I've always been detail-oriented, and I'm willing to follow any instructions you give me exactly. I'll do whatever it takes to make sure everything is perfect."

Mrs. Abreu leaned forward, her gaze sharp. "And what happens when you're asked to handle delicate tasks, like cleaning fine china or caring for expensive fabrics? Are you confident you can manage that?"

Lydia hesitates for a brief moment, but then his voice becomes firmer. "I've never worked with anything like that before, but I'm ready to learn. I'll be very careful, I promise. If you show me what needs to be done, I'll do it properly."

Mrs. Abreu sighed, flipping through the resume again. Mrs. Abreu tried to hide her smile as she read through the made-up life that she concocted for her husband. "This is a demanding job, Lydia. What makes you think you're up for it at this stage of your life?"

The question stang, but Lydia didn't let it show. He straightens in his chair, his voice soft yet pleading. "Because I need this, ma'am. I may be older, but I'm still capable. I can still work hard. I've been through tough times, but I'm not ready to give up. I'm committed to doing the best job possible. I need this chance."

A silence hangs between them as Mrs. Abreu studies Lydia's face. Hearing her husband beg felt delicious. Lydia felt the desperation rising in his chest, and before he can stop himself, he leans forward, his voice breaking slightly. "Please, ma'am. I'll do anything. I just need a chance. I can't afford to keep looking. I know I can do this job---I won't let you down. Please give me the opportunity."

Mrs. Abreu's eyes soften, though her expression remains reserved. She leans back in her chair, exhaling deeply. "Alright, Lydia. I'll give you a trial period, two weeks. If you can manage the work and meet my standards, the job is yours. But if not..."

Mrs. Abreu had no intention of ever firing Lydia. She wanted to see him on his knees doing her menial tasks. If he needed an attitude adjustment, the atozrocron was still available.

"I'll prove myself," Lydia interrupts, his relief palpable as he fights back tears. "Thank you. Thank you so much."

Mrs. Abreu nods, still a bit hesitant but resigned. "We'll see how it goes. Be here tomorrow morning at 8 a.m. sharp."

"I'll be here ma'am," Lydia promises, her voice trembling with gratitude as she stands up to leave.

On her first day, Lydia arrived at Mrs. Abreu's home promptly at 8 a.m., determined to make a good impression. The house was large and elegantly furnished, yet a tension seemed to hang in the air. Mrs. Abreu greeted him with a firm nod, showing Lydia around the house before giving him his first instructions.

"I need you to go through the house," Mrs. Abreu began, her voice clipped, "and remove anything that belonged to my ex-husband or that could possibly remind me of him. Pictures, clothes, books---anything at all. I want it gone. Throw it all out. After that, we'll focus on replacing what's missing."

Lydia, though surprised by the personal nature of the request, nodded dutifully. He had expected cleaning and tidying, but this task felt more emotional than physical. Still, he set about his work with determination.

Mrs. Abreu enjoyed the sight of her husband, dressed like a little girl playing maid, removing every trace of his former life from her house. She didn't have to tell him which items belonged to her husband, Lydia knew already.

As Lydia moved from room to room, he found every trace of Mrs. Abreu's ex-husband's items scattered throughout the house. A pair of leather-bound books on a shelf, an old sweater tucked into a drawer, and framed photos from vacations long past. Lydia hesitated for only a moment, wondering about the memories these objects held, before carefully placing each item into a large black garbage bag. He didn't want to dwell on the emotional weight behind the request---his job was clear. He was ruthlessly efficient in his task.

By the time he reached the living room, Lydia had filled dozens of bags with items from Mrs. Abreu ex-husband's former life in the home. Including all his apparel and personal items. There were golf trophies and cigars in an ornate box, a heavy armchair that Lydia knew he favored, and even small things, like a mug with his initials on it. Each piece was removed meticulously and without hesitation.

Lydia entered the dimly lit study which used to be his exclusive area. It is filled with dark, heavy furniture, and smelled faintly of cigars. Papers were still scattered on the large mahogany desk, and books lined the shelves. Lydia began sorting through the documents, placing them in neat piles, throwing out anything unnecessary. As he worked, he couldn't help but notice the personal nature of some of the items---letters, old photos tucked into books, mementos from a life that had once been shared in this very home.

Hours passed as he carefully boxed up the ex-husband's belongings. With each item he placed away, Lydia sensed the finality of it all---the erasure of a person's presence from a space they had once dominated. When he was done, he wiped down the desk, arranged the now-empty shelves, and took one last look at the room. It was stark, cold, no longer a reflection of the man who had once occupied it.

Lydia took a step back, surveying the now-empty spaces. The house felt lighter, less burdened by the remnants of a life now gone. When Lydia finished, Mrs. Abreu entered the room, her eyes scanning the emptiness.

"You've done well," Mrs. Abreu said, almost surprised at how quickly and thoroughly Lydia had performed the task. She gave a slight smile, a rare one, Lydia imagined. "Now, we need to fill those spaces."

The next task was to replace everything with items that gave the house a distinctly feminine touch. Mrs. Abreu led Lydia through her vision, pointing out where soft, pastel-toned throw pillows should be placed, and where scented candles and delicate vases filled with fresh flowers would make the house feel more hers. Lydia worked efficiently, moving through the rooms with care.

Mrs. Abreu could imagine what her unchanged husband would think about how feminine the house became. Knowing that he had made it that way himself was most gratifying.

Lydia replaced the heavy leather chair with a floral upholstered one. The bookshelf, now devoid of any trace of her ex-husband, was filled with glossy magazines, ornate picture frames, and decorative trinkets. Lydia hung flowing drapes in soft pinks and lavenders, replacing the heavy, masculine curtains that had once dominated the windows.

By the time he finished, Mrs. Abreu walked through the transformed house, her expression thoughtful and approving. The space was now filled with soft colors, intricate lace doilies, and gentle touches of femininity. Lydia could see how the changes lightened the atmosphere, giving the house a fresh, personal energy.

"You've done an excellent job, Lydia," Mrs. Abreu stated, standing in the center of the newly decorated living room. "I didn't expect you to handle it all so quickly. You have an eye for detail."

Lydia smiled, though inside, he felt the weight of the emotional task he had performed. "Thank you, ma'am," he said quietly, knowing this was just the beginning of his time in Mrs. Abreu's home.

As Lydia stepped back to admire the changes, he couldn't shake the feeling that he was helping erase someone's past entirely, piece by piece. But as Mrs. Abreu smiled, finally looking content in her surroundings, Lydia knew that this was what his employer needed---closure, or at least something close to it.

"Thank you, Lydia," Mrs. Abreu said, the tension in her voice softened by gratitude. "You've helped me more than you know."

Lydia, I'm sure you have put together many rooms, I want you to take that empty study and make it into a guestroom. I want it bright and airy, it is to be done in pastels. A place that ladies would feel at home in.

"I see."

"You can order anything you like. I don't care about the price. Just as long as the room is lovely."

"I understand Mrs. Abreu."

"Have the rug replaced and have the walls painted too."

"Yes, ma'am."

After Lydia finished transforming the guest bedroom with soft lavender walls, delicate lace curtains, and a plush, ivory bedspread, Mrs. Abreu inspected the space with a satisfied smile. The room was a far cry from its previous cold, unused state. It now radiated warmth and elegance, a perfect haven for any visitor.

Lydia stood by the door, nervous yet proud of his work. "I hope it's what you wanted, Mrs. Abreu."

Mrs. Abreu took a long look around, nodding approvingly. "You've done a beautiful job, Lydia. It's lovely, better than I imagined."

Relief washed over Lydia. "I'm glad you like it."

There was a pause, and then, unexpectedly, Mrs. Abreu turned to Lydia with a thoughtful expression. "You've been so devoted these past few weeks. I've seen how you take care of things, not just the house, but me, too."

Lydia wasn't sure what to say, so he simply nodded.

"I've been thinking," Mrs. Abreu continued, "this house is large and empty. It's a bit much for me, now that I'm on my own. But you, Lydia---you've made it feel less lonely. The way you've transformed this space has changed the entire atmosphere of my home."

Lydia smiled, unsure of where this was going. "Thank you, Mrs. Abreu. I'm happy to help."

"That's why," Mrs. Abreu said slowly, "I'd like you to consider moving in. You could stay in this room, the guest bedroom. It's already perfect. I wouldn't have to worry about hiring anyone else, and you'd have a stable place to stay. It would be convenient for both of us."

Lydia blinked in surprise. He hadn't expected this at all. "Move in?" he echoed, trying to wrap his head around the offer.

"Yes," Mrs. Abreu replied. "You've been so reliable, and I trust you. You've done more than any maid I've ever hired. It only makes sense."

Lydia hesitated. The offer was tempting. He didn't have much of a life outside of his work---his apartment was small, lonely, and his financial situation wasn't great. But moving in with his employer felt... strange. It blurred the line between professional and personal.

"I---well, I don't know what to say," Lydia stammered. "That's very generous of you."

"I understand if it's sudden," Mrs. Abreu said gently. "But think about it. You'll have more stability, and you won't have to commute. You'll have your own space, and you can continue to help me maintain the house."

Lydia bit her lip. "I don't want to impose..."

"You wouldn't be imposing," Mrs. Abreu assured him. "In fact, I think it would help both of us. You need a place, and I need someone I can rely on. It's a fair exchange. Truthfully, I just like having you around."

Lydia nodded, still processing the offer. It wasn't just about having a place to stay; it was about being a part of someone else's life in a more permanent way. Mrs. Abreu had clearly grown attached to Lydia, and while the idea of moving in was appealing, it also carried a sense of responsibility.

After a long pause, Lydia spoke. "I'll think about it, Mrs. Abreu. It's a kind offer, and I appreciate it."

"Of course," Mrs. Abreu said with a small smile. "Take your time. But I do hope you'll consider it seriously."

Lydia left the newly redecorated guest bedroom, his mind swirling with thoughts. He had no idea what the future held, but this was a turning point. Moving in could mean a fresh start, a new chapter in his life---but it would also mean becoming more entwined with Mrs. Abreu's world. As he walked down the hallway, Lydia realized that his decision could change everything.

Lydia hadn't any idea how much his life had changed already.
